## Where sources disagree — and how the site handles it

| Point | The disagreement | What the site does |
|---|---|---|
| Twin Bridges room count | 360 / 365 / 375 across sources | Says "khoảng 365 phòng" and notes the range |
| Twin Bridges nightly rate | Contemporary records say $8 + $1/person; Bill's own recollection is $9 | Uses Bill's figure, attributed to him, in the timeline detail |
| "Take care of your employees…" | Many oral variants; Marriott's own site uses a shorter form | Prints one form, attributes it to the **father**, and states openly that variants exist |
| Hotels visited per year | 120 / 200 / 250 / 265 / 320 depending on year and interview | Uses the verbatim "250" quote and notes the figure moved year to year |
| Starwood deal value | $12.2b initial / $13.3b / $13.6b final | Uses $13.6b (final agreed price) and explains the bidding sequence |
| Breach scale | "up to 500 million" initially, later ~383 million | Gives both, in that order, as the company itself did |

## Corrections made to the research leads in the brief

- **The boat fire was August 1985, not 1989.** Confirmed by the authorized biography and independently by UPI's contemporaneous report. It occurred eleven days after J.W. Marriott Sr.'s death — which is the whole meaning of the scene, and would have been lost at the wrong date.
- **Donna Garff Marriott died 30 December 2025, aged 90.** The brief was written while she was living. Section 10 was rewritten accordingly.
- **The famous "take care of your people" maxim belongs to the father,** not to Bill. The brief flagged this as needing verification; it does, and the site says so.
- **Courtyard launched June 1983**, after three years of development — not simply "~1983".
- **Marriott bought 49% of Ritz-Carlton in 1995**, not an unspecified "stake".
